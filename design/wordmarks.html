GUACA / WORDMARKS03   Conversation G

The icon starts the word.

The display face's own C, with the icon's reply bubble as the crossbar. The mark and the word stop being two things set side by side. In one color the bubble is just a crossbar, so it still reads as a G. The only one of the five that spends amber, and amber is the color that means the app wants something from you.

GUACA / WORDMARKS04   Eye contact

The two a's look at each other.

Drawn from circles and one round stroke, like the cast. Each a's bowl is an eye, and the pupils meet across the c: agents talking to agents, which is what the shipped icon says with two bodies. The pupils can move. Put the pointer on this page and they follow it; in the rail they would turn to the operator when something is waiting.

GUACA / WORDMARKS05   Crew

Five letters, five shapes.

Each letter is knocked out of one of the silhouettes every creature is cut from: circle, square, drop, cloud, octagon. The outlines are sampled from silhouette.ts, not traced, so the wordmark is the cast standing in a row. The loudest of the five, and the one that needs the most height to read.

GUACA / WORDMARKS06   Mashed

One mass, not five letters.

Literata Bold, softened by a round-joined stroke and kerned until the round letters touch. Straights keep a gap; rounds kiss. The most conventional of the five, and the one that holds up best at 16 pixels and on a favicon.

GUACA / WORDMARKS07   Drawn

Lettered with the cast's brush.

Every stroke goes through the drawn cast's own stroke(): heavy downstrokes, pointed ends, pigment printed off the line in the bowls. The rail cut uses a heavier line, which is what hint() does for a creature drawn small. Only fits a workspace that has chosen the drawn cast.
