Guaca icon

What ships today is three concentric ellipses in a green that no longer exists in the app: --flesh was ripe avocado and is now the signal amber. Primitive and off-brand are two separate problems, and only one of them is fixed by picking better colors.

The decision underneath is what the icon is of. The name says avocado. The app says a round creature made of clay whose eyes do the acting. The product says agents talking to agents. Each variant below picks one and commits. Pair was chosen: the app is the only one of the three that no competitor can copy, and two creatures aimed at each other say it without a word of explanation. Everything else here is kept as the record of what it was chosen over.

The creature variants are drawn by the app's own bodyPoints, outline and gaze math, ported from src/avatars/form.ts. They are the real species at rest, not a sketch of it. Every color is a token from src/styles.css; nothing here was invented.

The dock test — every variant at 64, 32 and 16

This is the row that settles it. A dock icon is judged at 64 among strangers and a menu-bar glyph at 16 in one color. Turn on Monochrome for the second of those. Anything that goes to mush here loses regardless of how it looks at 512.

Once one is picked it gets encoded in scripts/make-icon.py, the stdlib rasterizer that writes the 1024×1024 source cargo tauri icon derives every platform size from, and its outline half in scripts/make-tray.py. The tray glyph is already an egg outline, so a mark that is an egg keeps the menu bar and the dock speaking one language for free.
